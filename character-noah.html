Noah — He Condemned the World

“Noah was a righteous man. He proved himself faultless among his contemporaries. Noah walked with the true God.”
Genesis 6:9

Focus. Noah is the second man Genesis says walked with God. His courage took a shape Enoch's did not: decades of visible, laughable, back-breaking work in front of an audience that thought he was mad. Hebrews says he “condemned the world” — not by shouting at it, but by building.

1.Create context — when, who, and what was going on

When

Noah was born in 2970 BCE and was 600 years old when the Flood came in 2370 BCE. Jehovah's announcement that man's days would “amount to 120 years” (Gen. 6:3) was made around 2490 BCE — a 120-year countdown to the Flood.

Two details put the scale of his endurance in view. His sons were not born until he was about 500, so for most of the warning period he had a young family. And Methuselah — Enoch's son — died in the very year of the Flood, at 969.

Timeline — the 120-year countdown

3039 BCE Enoch taken by God, having foretold judgment (Jude 14, 15).
2970 BCE Noah born. Lamech names him, hoping for comfort from the cursed ground (Gen. 5:29).
2490 BCE The 120-year decree (Gen. 6:3). The clock starts.
2470 BCE Shem, Ham and Japheth born — Noah is about 500.
c. 2450 BCE The ark commission (Gen. 6:13, 14). Decades of construction begin.
2375 BCE Lamech, Noah's father, dies five years short of the Flood.
2370 BCE The Flood. Methuselah dies at 969 the same year. Eight people survive (1 Pet. 3:20).
2020 BCE Noah dies, aged 950.

What was going on

Two words define Noah's world. The first is wickedness: “every inclination of the thoughts of his heart was only bad all the time” (Gen. 6:5). That is total. The second is violence: the earth was “full of violence” (Gen. 6:13).

Yet Jesus adds a detail that is easy to miss. He does not describe Noah's neighbours as raging monsters but as people “eating and drinking, men marrying and women being given in marriage,” who “took no note” (Matt. 24:38, 39). Ordinary life, carried on with complete indifference to a warning being shouted in their hearing.

2.What was Jehovah’s view of the situation?
Heart of itJehovah was grieved rather than merely angry — and he looked hard enough at a corrupt world to find the one man in it who was not.

He was hurt by what he saw. “Jehovah regretted that he had made men on the earth, and his heart was saddened” (Gen. 6:6). This is not the language of a God who enjoys destroying.

He singled out one man for favour. “But Noah found favor in the eyes of Jehovah” (Gen. 6:8). Jehovah does not judge in blocks.

Genesis 6:5, 6

“Consequently, Jehovah saw that man's wickedness was great on the earth and that every inclination of the thoughts of his heart was only bad all the time. Jehovah regretted that he had made men on the earth, and his heart was saddened.”

He was patient far beyond what was deserved. Peter says “God was patiently waiting in Noah's day, while the ark was being constructed” (1 Pet. 3:20). The whole construction period was an extended opportunity to repent.

He closed the door himself. “Jehovah shut the door behind him” (Gen. 7:16) — a small phrase carrying enormous comfort. Noah did not have to decide when the waiting was over.

3.What was Noah doing — and how did he cope?
Heart of itHe preached with his hands. Decades of obedient work in public view were themselves the witness.

He obeyed exactly. The verdict is recorded twice: “Noah did according to all that God had commanded him. He did just so” (Gen. 6:22), and later, “Noah did everything that Jehovah had commanded him” (Gen. 7:5).

He preached as well as built. Peter calls him “a preacher of righteousness” (2 Pet. 2:5). He explained what he was doing and why, for decades, to people who laughed.

He “condemned the world.” Noah did not pronounce the sentence — God did. But his faith demonstrated that obedience was possible, which removed every excuse from everyone else (Heb. 11:7).

How did he cope?

  • He had godly fear. A healthy fear of displeasing God outweighed his fear of ridicule — he acted on “things not yet seen.”
    Hebrews 11:7 — “By faith Noah, after receiving divine warning of things not yet seen, showed godly fear and constructed an ark for the saving of his household”
  • He trusted instructions he could not fully evaluate. Nobody had seen rain like this, or a boat that size. He built anyway.
    Proverbs 3:5, 6 — “Trust in Jehovah with all your heart, And do not rely on your own understanding. In all your ways take notice of him, And he will make your paths straight.”
  • He had a definition of faith to live by — conviction about realities he could not yet see.
    Hebrews 11:1 — “Faith is the assured expectation of what is hoped for, the evident demonstration of realities that are not seen.”
  • He worked with his family. Unlike Enoch, Noah had allies under his own roof. Seven other people believed him. That is a small number against a world, but it is not nothing.
4.How can this account benefit me today?
Heart of itJesus said our own time would look like Noah's — not in its violence only, but in its indifference.
  • Take the warning seriously yourself first. Jesus' point is not mainly about the wicked; it is about people who took no note. The danger is drift, not open rebellion.
    Matthew 24:42 — “Keep on the watch, therefore, because you do not know on what day your Lord is coming.”
  • Doing the work is the witness. Noah's obedience preached before he opened his mouth. A life visibly ordered around Jehovah's standards argues for itself.
    Colossians 3:23 — “Whatever you are doing, work at it whole-souled as for Jehovah, and not for men,”
  • Ridicule is survivable, and temporary. Noah was a joke for decades, and right the whole time. Suffering for doing right is not a sign you have gone wrong.
    1 Peter 3:14 — “But even if you should suffer for the sake of righteousness, you are happy. However, do not fear what they fear, nor be disturbed.”
  • Obey the details. “He did just so.” Direction from Jehovah does not need improving upon, even when the reasons are not obvious yet.
    Luke 16:10 — “The person faithful in what is least is faithful also in much, and the person unrighteous in what is least is unrighteous also in much.”
  • God carries the parts you cannot. Jehovah shut the door. Decisions about timing and about others' outcomes are simply not yours to make.
    Psalms 55:22 — “Throw your burden on Jehovah, And he will sustain you. Never will he allow the righteous one to fall.”
  • A small faithful household matters. Eight people carried the human race through. Never assume a small group standing firm is insignificant.

Ask yourself: If someone watched my week without hearing me say a word about my beliefs, what would my choices tell them about what I expect the future to hold?
