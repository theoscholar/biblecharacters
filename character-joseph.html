Joseph — He Resisted Temptation

“There is no one greater in this house than I am, and he has not withheld from me anything at all except you, because you are his wife. So how could I commit this great badness and actually sin against God?”
Genesis 39:9

Focus. Joseph was young, far from home, and the only person who would ever have known. He said no — not once, but day after day — and when saying no stopped working, he ran.

1.Create context — when, who, and what was going on

When

Joseph was 17 when his brothers sold him (Gen. 37:2), which places that in 1750 BCE. He was 30 when he stood before Pharaoh (Gen. 41:46), in 1737 BCE. The events of Genesis 39 fall in the years between — so he was somewhere in his late teens or twenties, alone in a foreign country.

Two features of his situation are worth naming. He was a slave, so refusing his master's wife carried obvious danger. And he was completely without family oversight — no father, no community, nobody from home who would ever hear about it.

What was going on

Genesis is careful to establish that Joseph was doing well before the trouble started. “Jehovah was with Joseph. As a result, he became successful” (Gen. 39:2), and Potiphar “left everything that was his in Joseph's care” (Gen. 39:6). He had earned real trust.

Which is exactly what made the temptation dangerous. He had access, privacy and authority. And the pressure was not a single dramatic moment — “day after day she spoke to Joseph” (Gen. 39:10). This was a campaign, not an incident.

2.What was Jehovah’s view of the situation?

Heart of itJehovah's presence is the refrain of the whole chapter — and it does not stop when Joseph is punished for doing right.

God was with him in the house. The chapter opens with it twice: “Jehovah was with Joseph” and “his master saw that Jehovah was with him” (Gen. 39:2, 3). Even an Egyptian idolater could see it.

God was still with him in prison. This is the striking part. Joseph did the right thing and was jailed for it — and the account immediately says: “But Jehovah continued with Joseph and kept showing loyal love to him” (Gen. 39:21). The blessing did not depend on the circumstances improving.

Genesis 39:21

“But Jehovah continued with Joseph and kept showing loyal love to him and granting him favor in the eyes of the chief officer of the prison.”

He shared Joseph's view of what was at stake. Joseph called it “this great badness” and a sin “against God” (Gen. 39:9). He understood that the primary injured party would be Jehovah, not Potiphar.

3.What was Joseph doing — and how did he cope?

Heart of itHe had worked out his answer before he needed it — and when reasoning was no longer enough, he left the building.

He refused, and gave his reasons. The reply in verses 8 and 9 is not a panicked no. It is an argument: his master trusted him completely, the one thing withheld was his wife, and betraying that would be a sin against God (Gen. 39:8, 9). He had thought this through beforehand.

He named the real offence. He could have said it would be unfair to Potiphar — true, but secondary. He said “sin against God.” That is the category that held when nobody else was watching.

He kept refusing. “So day after day she spoke to Joseph, but he never consented to lie with her or to remain with her” (Gen. 39:10). Note the second half. He also avoided being alone in her company — he managed the situation, not just the moment.

He ran. When it became physical, he did not stay to argue: “he left his garment in her hand and fled outside” (Gen. 39:12). He accepted looking ridiculous, and losing the clothing that would be used as evidence against him, rather than stay one second longer.

He kept working afterwards. In prison he did not sulk. Before long “the chief officer of the prison put Joseph in charge of all the prisoners” (Gen. 39:22).

How did he cope?

  • He had decided in advance. The speed and clarity of his answer show a conclusion already reached.
  • He kept God in the picture. Being far from family did not mean being unobserved.
  • He removed himself. Refusing to “remain with her” is practical avoidance, not just willpower.
  • He did not let injustice make him bitter. The record of his conduct in prison is identical to his conduct in the house.

4.How can this account benefit me today?

Heart of itDecide before. Then, when the moment comes, you are executing a decision rather than making one.
  • Settle the question in advance. Joseph was not weighing options in the moment. Anyone deciding at the point of temptation is deciding at their weakest.
  • Name the real issue. “Sin against God” is a category that holds when nobody would find out and nobody would be hurt.
  • Pressure that repeats needs an answer that repeats. Day after day. A single firm no is not the same as a settled position.
  • Avoid the situation, not just the act. He would not even remain in her company. That is not weakness; it is strategy.
  • Sometimes the right thing costs you. Joseph went from steward to prisoner for doing right, and the account does not pretend otherwise.
  • Jehovah stays with you in the consequences. The same sentence that describes his imprisonment describes God's loyal love. That is the point of the chapter.
  • Running away is allowed. Fleeing is not cowardice when the alternative is negotiating with something that should not be negotiated with.

Ask yourself: What have I already decided about, in advance — and where am I still planning to work it out in the moment?
