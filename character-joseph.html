Joseph — He Resisted Temptation

“There is no one greater in this house than I am, and he has not withheld from me anything at all except you, because you are his wife. So how could I commit this great badness and actually sin against God?”
Genesis 39:9

Focus. Joseph was young, far from home, and the only person who would ever have known. He said no — not once, but day after day — and when saying no stopped working, he ran.

1.Create context — when, who, and what was going on

When

Joseph was 17 when his brothers sold him (Gen. 37:2), placing that in 1750 BCE. He was 30 when he stood before Pharaoh (Gen. 41:46), in 1737 BCE. Genesis 39 falls in the years between.

Two features of his situation matter. He was a slave, so refusing his master's wife carried obvious danger. And he was completely without family oversight — no father, no community, nobody from home who would ever hear about it.

What was going on

Genesis establishes that Joseph was doing well before the trouble started. “Jehovah was with Joseph. As a result, he became successful” (Gen. 39:2), and Potiphar “left everything that was his in Joseph's care” (Gen. 39:6).

Which is exactly what made the temptation dangerous. He had access, privacy and authority. And the pressure was not a single dramatic moment — “day after day she spoke to Joseph” (Gen. 39:10). This was a campaign, not an incident.

2.What was Jehovah’s view of the situation?
Heart of itJehovah's presence is the refrain of the whole chapter — and it does not stop when Joseph is punished for doing right.

God was with him in the house (Gen. 39:2, 3). Even an Egyptian idolater could see it.

God was still with him in prison. This is the striking part. Joseph did the right thing and was jailed for it — and the account immediately says Jehovah “continued with Joseph” (Gen. 39:21). The blessing did not depend on the circumstances improving.

Genesis 39:21

“But Jehovah continued with Joseph and kept showing loyal love to him and granting him favor in the eyes of the chief officer of the prison.”

He shared Joseph's view of what was at stake. Joseph called it a sin “against God” (Gen. 39:9). He understood that the primary injured party would be Jehovah, not Potiphar.

3.What was Joseph doing — and how did he cope?
Heart of itHe had worked out his answer before he needed it — and when reasoning was no longer enough, he left the building.

He refused, and gave his reasons. The reply is not a panicked no. It is an argument (Gen. 39:8, 9). He had thought this through beforehand.

He named the real offence. He could have said it would be unfair to Potiphar — true, but secondary. He said “sin against God.” That is the category that held when nobody else was watching.

He kept refusing. “he never consented to lie with her or to remain with her” (Gen. 39:10). Note the second half. He managed the situation, not just the moment.

He ran. “he left his garment in her hand and fled outside” (Gen. 39:12). He accepted looking ridiculous, and losing the clothing that would be used as evidence against him, rather than stay one second longer.

How did he cope?

  • He guarded his heart in advance. The decision was made long before the opportunity arrived.
    Proverbs 4:23 — “Above all the things that you guard, safeguard your heart, For out of it are the sources of life.”
  • He had settled what he would not look at. Job describes the same deliberate, prior commitment.
    Job 31:1 — “I have made a covenant with my eyes. So how could I show improper attention to a virgin?”
  • He fled rather than negotiated. The Bible's counsel on this temptation is not to stand and reason but to leave.
    1 Corinthians 6:18 — “Flee from sexual immorality! Every other sin that a man may commit is outside his body, but whoever practices sexual immorality is sinning against his own body.”
  • He took the way out. The open door was there, and he went through it instead of lingering.
    1 Corinthians 10:13 — “But God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear, but along with the temptation he will also make the way out so that you may be able to endure it.”
4.How can this account benefit me today?
Heart of itDecide before. Then, when the moment comes, you are executing a decision rather than making one.
  • Settle the question in advance. Anyone deciding at the point of temptation is deciding at their weakest.
    Proverbs 4:23 — “Above all the things that you guard, safeguard your heart, For out of it are the sources of life.”
  • Running away is allowed. Fleeing is not cowardice when the alternative is negotiating with something that should not be negotiated with.
    2 Timothy 2:22 — “So flee from youthful desires, but pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace, along with those who call on the Lord out of a clean heart.”
  • There is always a way out. Joseph's was a door. Yours may be a different room, a different app, a different friendship — but it exists.
    1 Corinthians 10:13 — “But God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear, but along with the temptation he will also make the way out so that you may be able to endure it.”
  • Sometimes the right thing costs you. Joseph went from steward to prisoner for doing right, and the account does not pretend otherwise.
    1 Peter 3:14 — “But even if you should suffer for the sake of righteousness, you are happy. However, do not fear what they fear, nor be disturbed.”
  • Jehovah stays with you in the consequences. The same passage that describes his imprisonment describes God's loyal love.
    Genesis 39:23 — “for Jehovah was with Joseph and Jehovah made whatever he did successful.”
  • Name the real issue. “Sin against God” is a category that holds when nobody would find out and nobody would be hurt.
  • Pressure that repeats needs an answer that repeats. A single firm no is not the same as a settled position.

Ask yourself: What have I already decided about, in advance — and where am I still planning to work it out in the moment?
