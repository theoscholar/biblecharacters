Enoch — He Walked With God

“Enoch kept walking with the true God. Then he was no more, for God took him.”
Genesis 5:24

Focus. Only two men in the whole Bible are described as walking with God. Enoch is the first. He was not a soldier and he fought no battle, yet he is a study in courage — because he spent his entire adult life saying an unwelcome thing to people who did not want to hear it, and he did it alone.

1.Create context — when, who, and what was going on

When

Enoch was born in 3404 BCE and was taken by God in 3039 BCE, at the age of 365 (Gen. 5:23). That places him seven generations after Adam — Jude calls him “the seventh one in line from Adam” (Jude 14).

Two dates are worth holding on to. Adam did not die until 3096 BCE, which means Enoch shared the earth with the first man for over 300 years. And the Flood did not arrive until 2370 BCE — nearly 700 years after Enoch was gone. Enoch announced a judgment he would never see arrive.

Timeline — Enoch in the stream of events

4026 BCE Adam created.
3896 BCE Seth born; the faithful line continues (Gen. 5:3).
3404 BCE Enoch born — seventh from Adam.
3339 BCE Methuselah born. From here Enoch “walked with the true God” for 300 years (Gen. 5:21, 22).

What was going on

Genesis is brief about Enoch's era, but Jude fills in the atmosphere. Enoch's contemporaries are described four times over in a single sentence as ungodly — in their deeds, in the manner of those deeds, and in the “shocking things” they said (Jude 15). This was not quiet indifference to God. It was loud contempt, and Enoch was standing in the middle of it.

The world had not yet reached the state it would be in by Noah's day, when it was “full of violence” (Gen. 6:13) — but it was travelling in that direction, and Enoch could see the road.

2.What was Jehovah’s view of the situation?
Heart of itJehovah was not a distant observer. He noticed one man in a hostile generation, kept company with him, and made sure he was rewarded.

Jehovah counted Enoch as a companion. The phrase “walked with the true God” is remarkable. It is not “obeyed God” or “believed in God.” Walking is what you do with a friend, over time, in the same direction. Genesis uses that expression of only two men — Enoch, and later Noah (Gen. 6:9).

Jehovah told him so. Enoch did not have to guess whether he was pleasing God. Paul says that “before he was transferred he received the witness that he had pleased God well” (Heb. 11:5).

Hebrews 11:6

“Moreover, without faith it is impossible to please God well, for whoever approaches God must believe that he is and that he becomes the rewarder of those earnestly seeking him.”

Jehovah entrusted him with a message about the future. The prophecy Jude preserves is about a judgment that would come (Jude 14, 15). Notice that Enoch spoke of it as already accomplished. He was that certain.

Jehovah protected him at the end. Enoch lived a mere 365 years in an age when men lived to 900 and more. The plain reading is that his enemies were closing in and God spared him a violent death: “he was no more, for God took him” (Gen. 5:24).

3.What was Enoch doing — and how did he cope?
Heart of itHis courage was not one brave moment. It was three centuries of small, consistent, unfashionable choices.

He kept walking. Genesis stresses it twice — he “continued to walk” for 300 years, and he “kept walking” (Gen. 5:22, 24). Nobody walks 300 years by accident. This is the courage of stamina rather than the courage of a single heroic act.

He said the hard thing out loud. Enoch's message named his listeners' conduct for what it was. That is a very different matter from privately disapproving.

He raised a family at the same time. Genesis says he had sons and daughters (Gen. 5:22). He was doing all of this while being a husband and a father in a community that despised what he stood for.

How did he cope?

  • He kept Jehovah constantly in view. “Walking” is a picture of continual awareness of God, and the Psalms describe the same habit.
    Psalms 16:8 — “I keep Jehovah before me constantly. Because he is at my right hand, I will never be shaken.”
  • He was convinced God existed and noticed. Hebrews places this thought right beside Enoch's account: God becomes the rewarder of those who seek him.
    Hebrews 11:6 — “whoever approaches God must believe that he is and that he becomes the rewarder of those earnestly seeking him.”
  • He had the relationship before he had the assignment. The walking came first. What Jehovah asks of a person is fundamentally a way of walking.
    Micah 6:8 — “And what is Jehovah requiring of you? Only to exercise justice, to cherish loyalty, And to walk in modesty with your God!”
  • He measured his life by God's opinion, not his neighbours'. By every social measure of his day Enoch was a failure — nobody listened. By God's measure he had pleased God well.
4.How can this account benefit me today?
Heart of itCourage is usually a direction you keep walking in, not a leap you make once.
  • You do not have to see the results. Enoch died 669 years before his prophecy came true. If you are speaking up and nothing seems to change, you are in good company.
    Galatians 6:9 — “So let us not give up in doing what is fine, for in due time we will reap if we do not tire out.”
  • Consistency beats intensity. Three hundred years of ordinary faithful days is what Genesis chose to record. The righteous course gets brighter gradually, not suddenly.
    Proverbs 4:18 — “But the path of the righteous is like the bright morning light That grows brighter and brighter until full daylight.”
  • God notices before anyone else does. Enoch was told he pleased God. You may not get a prophecy, but the principle stands.
    Hebrews 6:10 — “For God is not unrighteous so as to forget your work and the love you showed for his name”
  • Being the only one is survivable. There is no hint that anyone joined Enoch. Standing alone is hard, but it is neither new nor disqualifying.
    2 Timothy 3:12 — “In fact, all those desiring to live with godly devotion in association with Christ Jesus will also be persecuted.”
  • Your effort is not wasted. Enoch's message looked entirely fruitless in his lifetime. It was recorded, preserved, and is still being read.
    1 Corinthians 15:58 — “be steadfast, immovable, always having plenty to do in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not in vain in connection with the Lord.”

Ask yourself: Which is harder for me — doing the right thing once when it costs a lot, or doing it every day when nobody notices?
